The Legal Landscape: Lawsuit vs. Rebate Act

Understanding the two primary paths to reclaiming tariff duties in 2026.

Two Paths to Recovery

The $166B refund pool is divided between those who participated in the Section 301 litigation (The Lawsuit) and those seeking relief through the newly proposed American Worker Rebate Act (The Rebate Act). While they share a goal, their mechanisms and eligibility criteria differ significantly.

Comparison of Paths

Feature Section 301 Lawsuit Rebate Act (S.2475)
Primary Target Large Importers / Corporations Small Businesses / Individuals
Requirement Must have filed in Court of Int'l Trade Must prove tariff-induced inflation impact
Timeline Immediate (Court-ordered) Legislative (Pending approval)

Which One Applies to You?

If you are a business that imported goods from China and paid List 3 or List 4A duties, you likely joined the lawsuit years ago. If you are a consumer or a domestic manufacturer that didn't import directly, your hope lies in the Rebate Act, which aims to distribute the collected tariff revenue back to those affected by higher prices.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim both?

Generally no. The Rebate Act includes provisions to prevent "double dipping" for entities already receiving court-ordered refunds.

What if the Rebate Act fails?

If S.2475 does not pass, the funds remain in the general treasury, and only the court-ordered refunds for active litigants will proceed.
