E-commerce Sellers: Refund FAQ

Selling on Amazon, eBay, or Shopify? Here is what you need to know about the 2026 Tariff Refund rollout.

Relief for Online Merchants

E-commerce sellers were disproportionately affected by Section 301 tariffs, often lacking the scale to absorb the costs. The 2026 refund program includes specific "Micro-importer" provisions to help online sellers recover duties paid on high-velocity items like electronics and home goods.

Eligibility Criteria for Sellers

Seller Type Primary Proof Eligibility
Direct Importer CBP Form 7501 Automatic (via ACE)
Dropshipper Vendor Invoices Requires Verification
FBA Sellers Import Records High (if IOR)

Are Amazon Fees Refundable?

No. The 2026 refund program only covers the "Section 301" duties paid directly to U.S. Customs. It does not cover marketplace referral fees, storage fees, or shipping costs, even if those costs rose due to trade tensions.

Frequently Asked Questions

I use a freight forwarder. Who gets the refund?

The refund is issued to the "Importer of Record" (IOR) listed on the CBP paperwork. If your forwarder filed as IOR on your behalf, you must coordinate with them to receive your portion.

What if I no longer sell on the platform?

As long as you paid the duties between 2022 and 2025 and your business entity is still legally active (or you are the successor), you can still claim the refund.
