IRS vs. CBP: Who Handles Your Refund?

Navigating the bureaucracy of two federal agencies. Understanding the division of labor in the 2026 refund process.

A Shared Responsibility

The 2026 US Tariff Refund is unique because it involves both U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). CBP manages the import data and duty verification, while the IRS handles the actual disbursement of funds to individuals and tax-related offsets for businesses.

Agency Role Comparison

When to Contact Each Agency

Contact CBP if you have questions about whether a specific shipment (entry summary) is eligible or if your duty calculations are being disputed. Contact the IRS if your refund has been "approved" but you haven't received the funds, or if you need to update your direct deposit information.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the IRS tax my tariff refund?

Generally, no. Since the refund is a return of overpaid duties (costs), it is typically not considered taxable income, though it may affect your "Cost of Goods Sold" deductions.

Can I track my refund on the IRS website?

Yes, the IRS is launching a specialized "Tariff Refund Tracker" tool in late April 2026 for this specific program.
