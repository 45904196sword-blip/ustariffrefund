Section 301: The Legal Foundation

Understanding the Trade Act of 1974 and the litigation that led to the $166B 2026 refund rollout.

Origins of the Conflict

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 grants the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) broad authority to investigate and respond to unfair foreign trade practices. In 2018, this was used to launch a massive tariff campaign against Chinese imports, which eventually faced legal challenges from over 6,000 U.S. companies.

Key Legal Milestones

Year Event Legal Impact
2018 Lists 1 & 2 Enacted Initial $50B in tariffs.
2020 HMT Case Filed Beginning of List 3/4A litigation.
2024 Supreme Court Ruling Mandates refund of "Procedurally Defective" duties.
2026 Administrative Rollout CBP/IRS begin large-scale disbursements.

The "APA" Violation

The core of the successful legal challenge was the Administrative Procedure Act (APA). The courts found that the USTR failed to adequately respond to thousands of public comments before enacting List 3 and List 4A tariffs, rendering those specific duty collections legally invalid and subject to refund.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Lists 1 and 2 being refunded?

No. Currently, only List 3 and List 4A duties are included in the 2026 refund program as the court found the procedural errors only applied to those later lists.

Who is the lead plaintiff in the lawsuit?

The litigation was a "mass-action" consolidation, with HMTX Industries and JYSK Bed'n Linen serving as representative plaintiffs for thousands of businesses.
