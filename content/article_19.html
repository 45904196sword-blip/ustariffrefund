Mastering the Refund Appeals Process

Was your claim denied or underpaid? Learn how to navigate the 2026 Appeals phase to secure your full entitlement.

Your Right to Protest

The 2026 Tariff Refund program provides a robust mechanism for challenging CBP decisions. If your refund amount is lower than expected or if certain entries were excluded, you have the legal right to file a "Protest" under 19 U.S.C. § 1514.

Appeals Lifecycle

Stage Action Deadline
1. Form 19 Submission Formal written objection to denial. 60 Days post-denial.
2. Administrative Review CBP officer re-evaluates the evidence. Approx. 90 Days.
3. CIT Appeal Taking the case to Court of Int'l Trade. 180 Days post-final denial.

Critical Evidence for Success

Most successful appeals hinge on "Classification Correction." If your item was denied because it was classified under an ineligible HTS code, you must provide technical specifications, product photos, or independent laboratory reports to prove it belongs in a refundable category.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a lawyer for an appeal?

While you can file a Form 19 yourself, a licensed customs broker or trade attorney is highly recommended for CIT-level appeals.

Will an appeal delay my other refunds?

No. CBP processes "Accepted" entries immediately; an appeal only affects the specific entries that are in dispute.
